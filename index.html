Isaias, model by model

Physics-based, statistical and AI models are all forecasting the same Gulf hurricane. This page shows where they agree, where they split, and whether the split is unusual. It uses NOAA's public model file and three seasons of verification.

The guidance

Every model agrees on the shape. Isaias strengthens for about a day, then weakens fast between +36 and +48 h as NHC's track takes the center inland over the north-central Gulf coast. The models disagree on two numbers: how strong the storm is at the coast, and how fast it falls apart over land.

Where they split

At the peak, the storm-scale physics models run highest

At +24 h the regional physics models span 87–101 kt, with the Navy's COAMPS-TC highest. The global physics, statistical and interpolated DeepMind aids sit between 75 and 84 kt. Regional hurricane models use grids of a few kilometers that can resolve the eyewall. Global models, at roughly 9–25 km, cannot, and they usually run weak at the peak. NHC's 95 kt is near the top of the range.

Over land, the AI aid weakens fastest and the coarse global models slowest

At +48 h the DeepMind aid (GDMI) is at 23 kt, the regional physics models are at 29–43 kt and the GFS is at 39 kt. The UK and Canadian global models are still at 62 and 66 kt. SHIPS, a statistical model with no land in it by design, holds 67 kt. Its land-aware version, Decay-SHIPS, drops to 48 kt. The 19 kt between those two is the land effect as that statistical scheme models it, which makes the pair a useful control.

Some of the intensity spread is really track spread. A model that brings the center ashore earlier, or farther west, also weakens it earlier. At +36 h HAFS-A puts the center at 29.3°N 88.0°W and the DeepMind aid at 29.7°N 87.2°W, about 85 km apart (Fig. 4).

Why the AI line sits low: an adjustment step

The DeepMind line on most spaghetti plots is GDMI, an interpolated aid. The raw model output (GDMN) arrives too late for the forecast cycle it would serve. NHC therefore takes the previous run and adjusts it to the storm's observed intensity at the start time.

For Isaias, the raw 06Z DeepMind run forecast 82 kt for 12Z. The observed intensity was 70 kt. After the adjustment, the 12Z aid runs 7–16 kt below the raw run through +48 h. The raw run peaks at 95 kt, the same as NHC's official forecast.

"AI is less aggressive" is partly an artifact of this adjustment. Compare raw and interpolated output before drawing conclusions about any model class.

Tracks set the timing

Intensity forecasts over land depend on when and where the center crosses the coast. The tracks below cluster tightly toward the Alabama and western Florida Panhandle coast. Their small east–west and timing differences explain part of the +36 to +48 h intensity spread.

Is this disagreement unusual?

Not among the core models. The split comes from the models outside that core.

We took eight aids that ran in every season from 2023 to 2025: HAFS-A, HAFS-B, HWRF, HMON, COAMPS-TC, GFS, Decay-SHIPS and LGEM. For every Atlantic forecast cycle that started at hurricane strength, we computed their spread, the standard deviation across models. By that measure, Isaias's disagreement is ordinary at +24, +48 and +72 h.

Isaias stands out once the other models come back in. Adding the DeepMind aid, the UK and Canadian global models and SHIPS raises the +48 h spread from 6.8 kt to 14.3 kt. That figure uses a different set of models, so it can't be compared to the histograms. It does show where the disagreement comes from: models that treat land very differently.

The ensemble view

NOAA's global ensemble (GEFS) runs the GFS 31 times from slightly different starting conditions. At this resolution its intensities are much too weak. At 06Z the members started between 38 and 72 kt against an observed 70 kt, so read the plume for timing, not strength. 25 of the 31 members lose 15 kt or more between +36 and +48 h on the 12Z clock. That is independent evidence that the timing of the move inland is fairly well constrained.

The European (ECMWF) model and ensemble are not in NOAA's public model file, so they are not shown. Google's model is itself an ensemble, but the public file carries one forecast per cycle.

Scored after landfall

This page is a snapshot of the 12Z Thursday cycle, committed to a public repository before landfall, and the commit history timestamps it. After the storm, each aid will be scored against NHC's best track from +12 to +72 h, on the questions this storm raises:

  • Did the regional physics models' higher peak verify, or did NHC's 95 kt?
  • Did Isaias weaken over land as fast as the DeepMind aid predicted, or as slowly as the UK and Canadian models?
  • Was the raw DeepMind run (GDMN) closer than its interpolated version (GDMI)?

One storm can't settle which model is better. NHC's annual verification reports are where models get judged. This page is a worked example of reading the guidance.
