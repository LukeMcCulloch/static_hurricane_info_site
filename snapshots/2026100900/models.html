Hurricane model key

Intensity plots on Tropical Tidbits and elsewhere show a dozen lines labeled with four-letter codes. This key sorts the common codes by how each model works.

Reading the code

Most aids on a spaghetti plot are early versions of slower models. A trailing I means the previous run (6 h old) has been shifted in time and adjusted to start at the storm's observed position and intensity. A trailing 2 means the same adjustment applied to a run 12 h old. HFAI and HFA2 are therefore the same model, one cycle apart. The raw, unadjusted output usually has its own code, for example GDMN for Google DeepMind or HFSA for HAFS-A.

Physics: solves the equations of fluid flow and thermodynamics on a grid. Statistical: regression on predictors from past storms. AI: a model learned from historical data, which solves no physical equations when it runs.

NHC official

The human forecast, informed by everything below. Plan around this one.

OFCL
NHC official forecast for the current advisory.
OFCI
The previous official forecast, interpolated. This is the official line on the Tropical Tidbits intensity plot, so it lags the current advisory by one cycle.

Consensus

Averages of several models usually beat any single model.

IVCN
Intensity consensus: an average of several aids.
HCCA
HFIP Corrected Consensus Approach. A weighted average with each member's historical biases removed. In published evaluations it has been competitive with the best operational guidance.

Regional physics

Hurricane-specific models with nested grids that follow the storm (about 2 km for HAFS), coupled to an ocean model. They resolve the eyewall, so they are the physics models built to forecast peak intensity.

HFAI / HFA2 · HFBI
HAFS-A and HAFS-B, two configurations of NOAA's Hurricane Analysis and Forecast System. Operational since 2023.
HWFI / HWF2 · HMNI
HWRF and HMON, NOAA's previous-generation hurricane models, which HAFS replaced as the primary system. Their output still appears in NHC's 2026 model file.
CTCI
COAMPS-TC, the U.S. Navy's regional hurricane model.

Global physics

Whole-planet weather models. Strong on steering and track. With grid spacings of about 10 km or more (GFS about 13 km, ECMWF about 9 km) they can't resolve a tight inner core, so they usually forecast peak winds too low.

AVNI
GFS, NOAA's global model. AVN is its historical name.
AEMI
GEFS ensemble mean: the average of NOAA's 31-member global ensemble.
UKX2 / UKXI
UK Met Office global model.
CMC2 / CMCI
Environment and Climate Change Canada's global model (GEM).
CEM2 / CEMI
Canadian global ensemble mean.
EMXI
ECMWF, the European model. Shown on some plots, but not in NOAA's public model file.

Statistical & statistical-dynamical

Regression on environmental predictors such as wind shear, sea-surface temperature, ocean heat content and humidity, taken from GFS forecasts and fitted to decades of past storms. Hard to beat on average. They rarely predict rapid intensification.

SHIP
SHIPS. Has no land in it, so it stays strong after landfall by design.
DSHP
Decay-SHIPS. SHIPS with an empirical weakening rate over land.
LGEM
Logistic Growth Equation Model. The same predictors, with growth limited by the storm's theoretical maximum intensity.
OCD5
Climatology and persistence. A no-skill baseline: what history alone predicts.

AI & machine learning

Learned from historical data and very fast to run. They are new to operations, and their track record covers only a few seasons.

GDMI / GDM2 · GDMN
Google DeepMind's cyclone model (WeatherNext). NHC used it experimentally in 2025. Preliminary reviews, including one by retired NHC chief forecaster James Franklin, ranked it at or near the top of the guidance for track and intensity that season.
NNIC · NNIB
Neural-network intensity consensus. A small neural network that combines other aids (in 2021: HWFI, AVNI, DSHP and LGEM) with environmental predictors. NNIB is the equal-weight average of the same inputs, used as its baseline. Experimental.
