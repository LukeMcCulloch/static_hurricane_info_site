Hurricane Isaias Discussion Number 11, Fri 3:58 AM CDT (08:58Z).

The forecaster's reasoning (full discussion)

Isaias has intensified overnight. NOAA and Air Force Reserve Hurricane Hunter data showed that the maximum flight-level winds have increased up to 107 kt, with a better-defined eye and a shrinking eyewall. These changes are also present on satellite imagery, with a more symmetric presentation along with a warming eye/cooling eyewall. The available data suggest a current intensity of 95 kt for this advisory.

Isaias will likely become a major hurricane soon with the improving satellite imagery, and it could continue to intensify for the next 12 hours or so while it moves over very warm and deep water. However, a large increase in shear is anticipated before landfall, which will probably cause some weakening to occur. This is a tenuous spot because it would only take about a 6h delay in the onset of the shear to bring a stronger hurricane than expected onshore. The new forecast explicitly is a bit higher than the last one, but our best forecast at this time is for a high-end category 2 intensity at landfall, which is about the same as before. Regardless of its exact wind speed at landfall, Isaias will be a dangerous hurricane with significant storm surge, damaging winds, and flooding rains. The wind field is also predicted to grow due to a trough interaction, and some of these impacts will extend well away from the center. After landfall, gusty winds are expected to reach well inland over portions of the southeastern United States.

Isaias has turned towards the north-northeast (020/13 kt), and the hurricane is expected to turn northward and accelerate later today due to increasing flow between an upper trough near the northwestern Gulf Coast and a subtropical ridge extending across the western Atlantic. Landfall is expected to occur within the hurricane warning area Friday night or early Saturday. The new forecast is almost the same as before, very near the Google DeepMind guidance, which has been very accurate for Isaias.

ISAIAS BECOMES A MAJOR HURRICANE

PREPARATIONS WITHIN THE STORM SURGE AND HURRICANE WARNING AREAS SHOULD BE COMPLETED WITHIN THE NEXT FEW HOURS

Hurricane Isaias is now a major hurricane as it accelerates toward the north central Gulf Coast. Some weakening of the peak winds is possible just prior to landfall. However, Isaias is expected to still be a dangerous hurricane at landfall this evening across either coastal Alabama or the western Florida Panhandle. It is important not to focus just on the center as the wind field will expand by landfall, resulting in the potential for significant wind impacts and a life threatening storm surge along and to the east of where the hurricane makes landfall.

Peak storm surge inundation of 6 to 9 feet above normally dry ground is possible near and to the east of where the center makes landfall. Evacuation orders have been issued by some coastal counties and you should check with your local emergency management agency for the latest information.

Those in the hurricane warning need to prepare for damaging hurricane force winds of 74 to 110 mph, especially across coastal Alabama and the western Florida Panhandle. The strongest winds will occur near and just to the east of the center. Potential impacts from these winds include numerous downed trees and power lines, significant power outages, and some roof damage. Tropical storm force winds will spread well inland with hurricane force gusts likely into south central Alabama as the center of Isaias quickly moves north.

Rainfall totals of 4 to 8 inches are expected across southwest ALabama, south central Alabama, and the western Florida Panhandle. Localized amounts of 10 to 12 inches are possible in bands across the Florida Panhandle and near the center track of the storm. Areas of flooding are likely if the higher amounts are realized, with considerable impacts possible. Several rivers across the region are forecast to reach moderate flood stage with the potential of some rivers reaching major flood stage. In addition, a favorable environment for tornadoes will also develop to the east of the track as it moves inland Friday night through early Saturday morning. The greatest threat will be across the western Florida Panhandle and south central Alabama
