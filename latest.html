At 1000 PM CDT (0300 UTC), the center of Hurricane Isaias was located near latitude 25.4 North, longitude 88.4 West. Isaias is moving toward the northeast near 14 mph (22 km/h). A turn toward the north is expected on Friday. On the forecast track, Isaias is expected to approach the U.S. northern Gulf Coast on Friday and make landfall within the warning area late Friday or early Saturday.

Maximum sustained winds have increased to near 105 mph (165 km/h) with higher gusts. Isaias could briefly become a major hurricane early Friday. Although some weakening is forecast thereafter, Isaias will remain a dangerous hurricane as it approaches the northern Gulf Coast late Friday.

Hurricane Isaias Discussion Number 10, Thu 9:50 PM CDT (02:50Z).

The forecaster's reasoning (full discussion)

The satellite presentation of Isaias has improved over the past few hours. Infrared cloud top temperatures have cooled within the expanding central dense overcast, and recent images show a warm spot that could be an emerging eye. A series of earlier passive microwave images showed the mid-level eyewall was open to the southwest, and a comparison of the AMSR3 89- and 37-GHz channels indicated the vortex was slightly tilted with height due to continued southwesterly shear. The stronger Tail Doppler Radar winds noted from the previous mission indicated the satellite intensity estimates are likely running too low, and based on the improved structure of the hurricane, the initial intensity is raised to 90 kt. Air Force and NOAA Hurricane Hunter aircraft are scheduled to collect data in Isaias overnight.

Isaias is moving toward the northeast (040/12 kt), but is expected to turn northward and accelerate on Friday within the flow between an upper trough near the northwestern Gulf Coast and a subtropical ridge extending across the western Atlantic. Landfall is expected to occur within the hurricane warning area Friday night or early Saturday, with the updated 24-h forecast near the coast. The NHC forecast continues to lie closer to the right side of the guidance envelope, very near the latest corrected consensus (HCCA). This results in little overall change from the previous prediction, although this forecast is slightly faster based on the model trends this cycle.

High oceanic heat content and strong upper-level divergence could allow for additional strengthening overnight while the shear vector is still oriented in the direction of storm motion. A majority of Google DeepMind ensemble members show Isaias briefly reaching major hurricane status early Friday (along with some of the hurricane regional models), and this is now reflected in the NHC forecast. This strengthening should be short lived as southwesterly shear from the aforementioned trough will increase considerably on Friday and cause some weakening before landfall. Nonetheless, Isaias will be a dangerous hurricane with significant storm surge, damaging winds, and flooding rains when it reaches the northern Gulf Coast. During this time, the wind field is also predicted to grow due to the trough interaction, and some of these impacts will extend well away from the center. After landfall, gusty winds are expected to reach well inland over portions of the southeastern United States.

ISAIAS EXPECTED TO APPROACH THE NORTHERN GULF COAST AS A DANGEROUS HURRICANE ON FRIDAY

STORM SURGE AND HURRICANE WARNINGS REMAIN IN EFFECT

Hurricane Isaias is forecast to continue strengthening through Friday morning as it moves northeast into the central Gulf. The hurricane will then turn northward and accelerate toward the north central Gulf Coast. Some weakening of the peak winds is possible just prior to landfall. However, Isaias is expected to still be a dangerous hurricane at landfall Friday night. It is too early to pinpoint the landfall location, but it is expected across either coastal Alabama or the western Florida Panhandle. It is important not to focus just on the center as the wind field will expand by landfall, resulting in the potential for significant wind impacts and a life threatening storm surge along and to the east of where the hurricane makes landfall.

Peak storm surge inundation of 5 to 7 feet above normally dry ground is possible near and to the east of where the center makes landfall. Areas that are west of the center will see a strong north wind and below normal water levels. Evacuation orders have been issued by some coastal counties and you should check with your local emergency management agency for the latest information.

Those in the hurricane warning need to prepare for damaging hurricane force winds of 74 to 110 mph, especially across the western Florida Panhandle. The strongest winds will occur near and just to the east of the center. Potential impacts from these winds include numerous downed trees and power lines, significant power outages, and some roof damage. Tropical storm force winds are likely to spread well inland, at least in frequent gusts, as the center of Isaias quickly moves north.

Rainfall totals of 4 to 8 inches are expected across southwest ALabama, south central Alabama, and the western Florida Panhandle. Localized amounts of 10 to 15 inches are possible in bands across the Florida Panhandle and near the center track of the storm. Areas of flooding are likely if the higher amounts are realized, with considerable impacts possible. Several rivers across the region are forecast to reach minor to moderate flood stage. In addition, a favorable environment for tornadoes will also develop to the east of the track as it moves inland Friday night through early Saturday morning. The greatest threat will be across the western Florida Panhandle and south central Alabama.
